# Video Editor: installable PWA

A browser video editor for YouTube videos and Shorts. It runs entirely on your device, works offline once installed, and saves your projects automatically, including the video files.

The app is plain static files (HTML, CSS and ES modules). There's no build step. You can host the folder on any static host that serves HTTPS.

## Features
- **Timeline.** Whole-sequence playback with a playhead. Scrub on the ruler, zoom (buttons, slider, Ctrl+wheel, `+`/`-`/`0`), and use the separate **Video / PiP / Text / Audio** tracks. Music and voiceover tracks show waveforms (video clips show thumbnails), and keyframes show as ◆ diamonds (click one to jump to it). Clip widths match their durations. Drag clips to reorder them and drag their edges to trim. Snapping and markers are included. Touch works too: tap an item to select it, then drag it or its edges.
- **Free-form lanes (like CapCut / Filmora / ElevenLabs Studio / Canva).** The timeline is one stack of plain, unlabelled lanes. Any item – a video clip, photo, overlay video, text, blur region, music, voiceover, caption – can sit on any lane at any time; items show what they are by their own look (thumbnail strip, waveform, text, coloured block), not by a row name. Drag **sideways** to change the start time, **up/down** to change lane; drop into the “New lane” strip above or below the stack to open a new lane (empty lanes close by themselves). A higher lane is drawn on top of a lower one, and the sound of every lane is mixed. A lane's speaker button (shown only when the lane has sound) mutes that whole lane. Dragging near something snaps to the playhead and to other items' edges (Snap button; hold **Alt** to move without snapping). **Ripple** on keeps the clips joined (dragging reorders them); off lets clips leave gaps. Dropping a clip over another clip turns it into a full-frame layer on its own lane (it keeps time, speed, volume and fades, but not transitions, colour grading, motion keyframes or fit). Arrow keys nudge the selected item (Shift = bigger steps); on a phone use the pinch gesture to zoom. Limits: captions cannot overlap each other in time (only one caption line is on screen), items on the same lane cannot overlap (the item moves to the nearest free spot or to a new lane), and one item is moved at a time. Old projects open unchanged: their items are placed on lanes in the same stacking order they were drawn in before.
- **Edit.** Split at the playhead (`S` or the Split button) on whichever item is selected: a main clip, text layer, music or voice track, or overlay. With nothing selected it splits the main track. Keyframe and Ken Burns motion carry on smoothly across the cut, and trims, fades and loops are divided correctly. Also duplicate and delete (toolbar, Ctrl+D, Del) for every item type: clip, text, overlay, music, voice and marker. Split and Duplicate live only in the timeline toolbar and their shortcuts (the Clip, Text, Picture-in-picture and Look side panels no longer repeat them; the Projects list still has its own whole-project Duplicate). With nothing selected the buttons are dimmed and tapping one says what to select. Ripple (text, music and markers after an edit shift with it). Undo/redo covers every edit (Ctrl/Cmd+Z, Shift+Ctrl/Cmd+Z, Ctrl+Y).
- **Per clip:** clips are labelled with their file name on the timeline (there is no name field in the Clip tab; a name saved in an older project is kept), trim, speed from 0.25× to 4× (audio keeps its pitch in the export), volume up to 200%, a **Mute** toggle (silent in preview and export, and it never ducks music), audio fade in/out, transition in (cut, crossfade, fade to black), fit, background override (blurred copy, black, white), zoom/pan crop, free rotation, 90° rotate, flip, opacity, Ken Burns motion, color (brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, vignette) and presets (Warm, Cool, B&W, Vintage, Vivid, Dramatic, Golden hour).
- **Global look:** aspect ratio (Original, Widescreen 16:9, Vertical 9:16 for Shorts, Square 1:1, Portrait 4:5), fit, background (black, white, custom color, blurred video; switching to Shorts turns on the blurred fill automatically), a global color grade, an end fade, and a logo/watermark with position, size and opacity.
- **Image clips** (JPG/PNG/WebP/GIF/HEIC) for title cards, with an adjustable duration. Animated GIFs play in the preview and the export. HEIC/HEIF photos from iPhones are converted to JPEG on import: natively where the browser can decode them (Safari), otherwise with the bundled offline libheif decoder, which is only downloaded when needed.
- **Text layers:** add them from the Text tab ("＋ Text at playhead") or with the `T` key; as many as you want. Each one has its own text, start/end, position (drag it on the preview or use presets), size, scale, rotation, opacity, width, color, style (Clean, Band, Box, Outline), font (bundled IBM Plex family), alignment, and fade in/out.
- **Animated text presets:** Typewriter, Slide up, Pop and Word-by-word in-animations, plus Fade, Slide down, Pop out and Un-type out-animations, each with its own duration.
- **Keyframes:** animate position, scale, rotation and opacity of main clips, text layers and overlays (volume has its own envelope, see below). Easing can be Linear, Ease in, Ease out, Ease in/out or Hold. Add a keyframe with the ◆ button in the timeline toolbar (or Shift+K); it works while a clip, text layer, overlay, blur region or music/voice track is selected (on a music or voice track it adds a volume keyframe). On anything else the button is dimmed and tapping it tells you what to select. After that, moving a slider or dragging the item on the preview sets a keyframe at the playhead automatically.
- **Picture-in-picture (PiP tab):** a second video or image over the main track. It has its own timing and trim, corner/center/full presets, position, size, scale, rotation, opacity, rounded corners, border, shadow, fades (the overlay's sound fades too), speed from 0.25× to 4× for video overlays (the sound keeps its pitch), and optional sound.
- **Chroma key (green screen)** for overlays: a key color (with "Pick from preview"), similarity, smoothness and spill suppression. It runs in a WebGL shader in both preview and export.
- **Voiceover:** record from the microphone straight into a voice track at the playhead. It shows a live level meter and a timer, and after each take you can listen back, then keep it, retake it or discard it. The video can play (muted) while you record. Music ducks under voice tracks automatically. Shortcut: `R` starts a 3-second countdown (press `R` or `Esc` again to cancel), and `R` during a take stops it. The microphone is released as soon as a take ends.
- **Connect tab (integrations):** a browser page cannot control other apps or websites (and embedding sites such as Canva or YouTube in a panel doesn't work, because they refuse to be framed), so this tab only moves files in and out. (1) **Receive files from other apps:** once the editor is installed, Share in Photos, Files, Drive or a browser lists Video Editor (Web Share Target, Chromium browsers on Android and desktop; Safari on iPhone does not support it, use the picker button instead). Videos and photos go on the main track, audio on the music track, and a shared link fills the link box. It works offline once installed, and an editor window that is already open is told by the service worker. (2) **Import from a link:** paste an `https://` address of a direct video, photo or audio file. It is fetched with CORS and no credentials, with a progress bar (size and percent), Cancel, and plain explanations for a site that blocks other websites (CORS), a web page instead of a file, 404/403, an empty or oversize file (1 GB, 300 MB on phones), a non-https redirect, or being offline. Only the link is fetched (GET); nothing from your project is sent anywhere. (3) **Share out:** Share buttons after an export and in the Thumbnail maker use the system share sheet with the file where supported (`navigator.share`), otherwise download it. **Add from Drive, Photos or Files** is a plain file picker accepting `video/*,image/*,audio/*`, which is what makes Android's system picker offer Drive and Photos.
- **Blur / Privacy (Look tab):** tap **＋ Blur region** to hide a face, a screen or a license plate, or to blur the background. A region is its own timed item on a **BLUR** track (like a text layer), so you can move it, trim it, snap it, split it, duplicate it, delete it and undo all of that. Shape: box (with a corner-radius slider) or ellipse. Effect: **Blur** or **Pixelate** (mosaic), with a strength slider, a soft-edge (feather) slider, and fade in / out. **Blur everything outside the shape** turns it into a background blur / spotlight. Drag the box on the preview and pull its round handles (44 px touch targets) to resize; arrow keys nudge it and Alt+arrows resize it. To follow a moving face or object, tap **◆ Keyframe at playhead**, move the playhead, then drag the box again: position and size are keyframed with easing. Split, Ripple, Duplicate and the thumbnail maker all include it, and the picture is identical in the preview and in both export engines (WebCodecs MP4 and MediaRecorder). **Limits:** there is no automatic face or object tracking (you set the keyframes yourself), and at most 12 blur effects are drawn on one frame. Clip tab › **Blur background** blurs a whole clip, optionally keeping a sharp draggable subject area.
- **Volume keyframes (volume envelope):** volume can be keyframed on main clips, overlay videos, music and voice tracks. The envelope is a **multiplier of the Volume slider** (100% = the slider's level, up to 200%), so the slider stays the overall level; fades, ducking and mute apply on top. Tap **Keyframe** (toolbar or `Shift+K`) with a music or voice track selected to add a volume keyframe at the playhead. For clips and overlays the Clip / Picture-in-picture tab has a **Volume envelope** section next to the position / scale / rotation / opacity keyframes (motion keyframes and volume keyframes are separate: the motion button never adds volume keys). Each key is listed with its time, level (%) and easing (Linear, Ease in/out, Hold) and can be deleted, and **Clear volume keys** removes them. On the timeline the selected item shows a yellow line over the waveform (clips and overlays: over the picture; the middle is 100%, the top 200%) with dots you can drag with the mouse or a finger: sideways to move a key in time, up and down to change its level (5% steps, hold Shift for fine). The envelope is applied sample-accurately in both export engines (WebAudio gain automation in the offline mixer, sampled every 20 ms and exactly at each key, so a Hold key steps within half a millisecond) and with a 100 ms-lookahead WebAudio ramp in the preview. Keys keep their place on the sound when you split (the level is interpolated at the cut), duplicate, trim the start (or drag the left edge) or move the item; looped music applies the envelope over its whole looped length; a clip whose envelope is silent does not duck music there. Saved projects: keys live in `keyframes.volume` (no schema change); older projects load with no envelope, and imported keys are cleaned (finite numbers, 0–2, valid easing, one key per moment).
- **Detach audio:** select a video clip (Clip tab) or a video overlay (Picture-in-picture tab) and tap **Detach audio**. It creates a new track in the Audio tab with that clip's sound (same start, in-point, length, speed, volume, fades and volume envelope; picture transitions become audio fades) and mutes the original, so the sound is exported exactly once. It reads the same stored media, streamed like music and voice (WebCodecs, a few seconds in memory at a time; the preview plays it from the same file), so a 60-minute source is never decoded whole. After detaching, the two are independent: the new track can be moved, trimmed, split, faded, keyframed, muted or removed on its own (undo restores the clip's sound and removes the track in one step). Detaching a muted or already detached clip explains why nothing happened. There is no toolbar button for it, on purpose. The button answers at once (it shows **Detaching…** with a spinner and ignores extra taps); the new track is created instantly without decoding the file, and the waveform fills in later in the background. A quick header-only check (5 s hard timeout) then tells you if the video has no audio ("This video has no audio": the detach is undone; for known silent videos the button stays visible but greyed with the reason) or if your browser can't read its audio format (the track is still created and refers to the original media). Any error in a button shows as a toast instead of failing silently.
- **Extract audio (save the sound as a file):** Clip tab / overlay panel → **Extract audio as a file…**, the **Extract audio** button in the timeline toolbar, **Save as audio file…** on a music/voice track, **Audio tab → Extract audio from a video file…** (any video or audio file, even one the browser can't show, e.g. iPhone H.265 on a laptop) and **Export audio only** (the whole timeline mixed, no picture) next to Export video. Formats: **M4A** (an AAC track is copied without re-encoding, so an hour takes seconds), **Opus (.ogg)** and **WAV**. It streams to disk in small pieces with a progress bar and **Cancel**; closing the sheet cancels; a stalled job ends with a clear message after 45 s. Messages: "This video has no audio.", "This audio format can’t be read by your browser (ac3).".
- **Match audio (timeline toolbar):** select an image clip (or a GIF/still) and tap **Match audio** to make it end where the audio ends. Which audio: a music, voice or detached-audio track that overlaps the image's start or starts within half a second after it (a voice/detached track wins over music, then the closest start); otherwise the unmuted track that ends last. Length on the timeline accounts for trim, speed and a looped track's set length; the image length is rounded up to a whole frame (project fps) so the picture never ends before the sound. A toast says what was matched ("Image now 02:34, matching Voiceover"). With an audio track selected instead, it matches the image under that track's start. Ripple follows the Ripple toggle (later text, music, overlays and markers shift; with Ripple off nothing else moves and clips follow the normal overlap rules). Keyframes keep their relative place (times scale with the new length, end values are unchanged) and Ken Burns spreads over the new length. One undo step; a second tap says it already matches. A video clip can't be stretched (the toast says so and points to Speed), a looped track with no set length repeats to the end of the video so it has no end to match (the toast says to set its length), and with nothing selected or no audio the button is dimmed (`aria-disabled`) and tapping it explains. On phones it is a 44 px icon in the scrolling toolbar; on desktop it shows the "Match audio" label. Saved projects need no change (it only edits the image's `out`).
- **Volume down / Volume up (timeline toolbar, `[` and `]`):** two buttons (speaker-minus / speaker-plus icons, 44 px on phones, in the scrolling toolbar) change the **base Volume** (the same value as the Volume slider; volume keyframes are not touched) of the selected main clip, overlay video, music track or voice / detached-audio track by **10 percentage points** per tap; hold **Shift** or **Alt** while tapping or pressing the key for **1-point** steps. The level is clamped to 0% up to the slider's own maximum (200% for clips and overlays, 150% for music and voice) and a toast shows the result ("Volume 120%", "(max)", "(silent)", or "already at the maximum / already 0%"). The inspector slider and its readout follow, and during playback the audio changes at once. A burst of taps (each within 1 s of the last, on the same item) is **one undo step**. Dimmed (`aria-disabled`, still tappable) with nothing selected, an image, a video with no audio, text, a blur region or a marker, and the toast says why; a muted item says it is muted and where its Mute button is, instead of changing silently. Hold-to-repeat is not implemented on the buttons (the `[` / `]` keys repeat while held). Saved projects are unchanged (only `volume` is edited). Listed in the shortcuts help (`?`).
- **Captions (Captions tab, `CAPS` row on the timeline):** one project-wide list of timed captions with a single style, burned into every export (MP4 and WebM, streaming included) and shown in the preview. The CAPS row appears once there are captions: tap a block to select it (the editor panel opens with its text and start/end), drag it to move, drag its edges to trim, **Split** (`S`) cuts it at the playhead, **Delete** removes it, Duplicate copies it, and ‹ › in the panel jump between captions. **＋ Caption at playhead** adds one by hand. **Style:** four presets (Classic with a dark box, Bold Shorts, Highlight word, Minimal) and a *Customize* section (font, size, text/outline/box/highlight colors, outline thickness, top/middle/bottom plus a raise slider, words per caption, max lines, ALL CAPS, highlight the spoken word). Text always wraps inside the frame (a very long caption shrinks a little, an unbroken 100-character word is broken up). On 9:16 the captions sit well above the bottom 20% where YouTube Shorts puts its own buttons. Changing *words per caption* (or choosing a preset that has a different value) re-splits every caption (one undo step, text and word timings are kept). **Import .srt** (also reads `.vtt`), **Download .srt**, **Clear all** and **Fix a word everywhere** (whole-word, case-insensitive find and replace) are in the same tab. Captions are in timeline time: they move with Ripple edits (a ripple delete or a speed change that shortens the timeline shifts the captions after it), but if you rearrange clips afterwards, use **Re-transcribe…** or drag them yourself. Captions are part of undo/redo, autosave and the `.vedit` project file. Hour-long timelines are fine: only the visible captions are drawn on the timeline.
- **Auto-transcribe (on device):** **✨ Generate…** in the Captions tab runs OpenAI Whisper in your browser (nothing is uploaded, no account, no server). The library is `@huggingface/transformers` 3.8.1 with onnxruntime-web, vendored in `vendor/whisper/`, and it runs in a Web Worker so the editor stays responsive. The model files (8-bit ONNX, from Hugging Face `Xenova/whisper-*`) are downloaded **once**, after a confirmation that states the size: **Fast = Whisper tiny, about 45 MB** (default, English uses `tiny.en`), **Better = Whisper base, about 81 MB**, plus the 22 MB WebAssembly runtime from this site. They are kept in Cache Storage (the app asks the browser to keep them), so later runs work fully offline; "Free up space" in the dialog removes them. The service worker never precaches the model or runtime (they would add about 100 MB to every app update); the runtime lives in its own cache that app updates do not delete. Languages: English (default), Auto-detect and 18 others (these use the multilingual models). It transcribes the speech of the whole timeline (clip sound, overlay sound, voiceovers, detached audio; music is skipped; muted clips are skipped), or only the selected clip, in timeline time, so trims, speed changes, several clips and gaps are already correct. Audio is streamed in 30-second windows at 16 kHz mono (an hour is never decoded into one buffer), each window resumes after the last reliable word, silent windows are skipped, and a window whose word timings collapse is re-run from the last good word. Progress shows percent, audio time and an estimate of the time left, with Cancel; the screen is kept awake while it runs. Word timings drive the *highlight word* style. **Names & terms:** this version of transformers.js has no prompt option for Whisper, so the optional list ("Jesus, Hallelujah, Deuteronomy") is applied *after* transcription: similar-sounding words are snapped to your spelling. Use **Fix a word everywhere** for anything else; everything is editable afterwards. Speed: roughly 5× real time for *Fast* on a laptop CPU (an hour in about 12 minutes) and several times slower on a phone, so for an hour-long sermon on a phone the dialog suggests generating on a laptop and importing the `.srt`. It runs on the CPU (WebAssembly, one thread); WebGPU is not used.
- **Clean voice (on device):** one control under the **Volume** slider of any selected sound (video clip, overlay video, music, voiceover, detached audio): **Off / Light / Strong**, plus **Hear the original** to compare. It removes hum, fans, room and crowd noise from speech for sermon recordings made on a phone or in a room. Nothing is uploaded. **Light** (RNNoise, ~125 KB, shipped with the app, works offline at once) is gentle and fast (about 30× faster than real time on a desktop). **Strong** (DPDFNet neural network, a one-time ~21 MB download that you confirm, then kept for offline use) removes far more but is slower (about 0.4× real time on a desktop; phones are slower). The sound is cleaned in 10-second pieces by a Web Worker, so an hour-long recording needs little memory, and the result is stored as a separate mono 48 kHz copy; **the original is never changed** and Off brings it back. Trim, speed, volume, fades, mute, keyframes, split/duplicate/move and lanes work as before; MP4 and WebM export, *Export audio only* and *Extract audio* use the cleaned sound. A `.vedit` file with media includes the cleaned copies; a `.vedit.json` (no media) keeps the setting and the control offers **Clean now**. Honest limits: it will not remove echo or reverb, and music playing under a voice is only reduced by Strong (Light treats it as part of the voice); very noisy sources can sound slightly processed; the waveform still shows the original; quality was measured on synthetic noise over a speech sample, not on a wide range of real rooms.
- **Responsiveness:** waveform work runs in small slices that yield to the page (and back off while the page is busy); one waveform job at a time; a long-task monitor (`app.perf`) watches for main-thread blocks. Waveforms/metadata are stored in a tiny IndexedDB entry (`mx:<id>`) instead of rewriting the whole video record. An idle installed tab takes a new version by itself; a tab with edits shows the "new version ready" bar.
- **Mute buttons:** every timeline track header (VIDEO, PIP, MUSIC and, once you have a voiceover, VOICE) has a speaker button (`aria-pressed`, at least 40 px to tap) that mutes or unmutes everything on that track in one tap. It shows a mixed state when only some items are muted. Muted clips, PiP videos and music/voice tracks get a small speaker-off badge on the timeline. It is undoable, saved with the project, and copies made by Split and Duplicate stay muted. Muted audio is left out of both export engines, and a muted clip or voiceover does not trigger ducking.
- **Music tracks:** trim, offset, volume, fade in/out, **Loop** (repeats the trimmed section until the video ends, or for a length you set; loop seams show on the timeline), and optional auto-ducking under clip audio and voiceovers. A track never ducks under itself. The timeline shows a waveform.
- **Starter templates:** Scripture verse card, Channel intro, Shorts quote (9:16), Lower-third name, and Sermon/devotional outline with section markers. Each one creates a new project or is inserted at the playhead, with generated backgrounds, animated text and slow-zoom keyframes. Everything stays editable.
- **Export:**
  - Settings: 720p, 1080p or 4K; 24, 30 or 60 fps; three quality levels; Auto, MP4 or WebM. **Auto** makes an MP4 where the browser can encode H.264 and otherwise a WebM (VP9/VP8), in both engines. The file extension always matches the container, and the file is named after the YouTube title, or the project name when that is empty (letters in any language, digits, dots and dashes are kept).
  - A progress bar shows the ETA and render speed, and you can cancel.
  - **Low memory use:** audio is decoded and mixed in 10-second chunks, and the output file streams to disk: straight into a file you pick ("Save straight to a file", Chrome/Edge desktop) or into the browser's private storage (OPFS) and then downloaded. Where neither is available the file is built in memory, and you get a warning before long exports on low-memory devices.
  - **Fast engine:** WebCodecs through the vendored [Mediabunny](https://mediabunny.dev) library (MPL-2.0). It decodes and encodes frame by frame, so it isn't tied to real time and audio/video sync is frame-accurate.
  - **Fallback engine:** a real-time canvas + MediaRecorder recording. It prefers MP4 when the browser supports it and falls back to WebM.
- **Thumbnail maker:** pick a frame, add a headline, a small line and an accent color, then download it as a JPG (kept under 2 MB, quality and size are lowered automatically if needed) or PNG. Choose the format: **Widescreen 16:9 (1280×720)**, **Vertical 9:16 for Shorts (1080×1920)** or **Square (1080×1080)**. It defaults to your project's aspect ratio. Text is laid out inside a safe area (shown as a dashed guide that is not saved in the image), shrinks to fit narrow Shorts widths, and the layout can be Left, Center, Right, Top band or Bottom band. Picture-in-picture overlays and the logo are included (each can be switched off). Files are named like `My-Video-thumbnail-shorts-1080x1920.jpg` (or `-widescreen-1280x720`, `-square-1080x1080`).
- **Projects:** the project and its media blobs autosave to IndexedDB. A status label shows *Unsaved changes*, *Saving…*, *Saved hh:mm* or *Save failed – retrying*, and failed saves retry automatically. Older saved projects are migrated to the current schema when they open. The project list lets you create, open, rename, duplicate and delete. Switching projects saves pending edits first and frees the previous project's decoded media. There is exactly one video export: **Export video** in the publish row at the bottom (the header has no export button). In the **Projects** list, each project has **Back up project** (tooltip “Save project file (.vedit)”), which saves the project file, not a video. You can back up a project as a small `.vedit.json` (media stays on the device) or, with “Include media files” ticked, as a `.vedit` file: a standard tar archive holding `project.json` plus each media file as raw bytes, streamed straight from storage (no base64, so it's about the size of the media). Older `.vedit.json` files with base64-embedded media still import. Media that wasn't embedded can be relinked. **Project names:** a new project is named like `Project · Sep 29, 8:52 PM` (locale aware, never “Untitled”). In the header, tap the name or the pencil to rename it in place: Enter or leaving the field saves, Esc cancels, an empty name goes back to the dated default, 80 characters at most, one undo step; long names are cut with an ellipsis. Projects saved as “Untitled project” (or with no name) are renamed from their created time the first time this version opens them; every other name is kept. The **Projects** list shows each project's name, clip count, length and last-edited time, and marks the current one. Export, thumbnail and share file names use the project name with unsafe characters removed.
- **PWA:**
  - A real manifest with icons, including maskable and Apple touch icons.
  - A service worker that caches the app shell for offline use under a versioned cache. The page and its scripts are always served from the same cached version. `bump-version.py` stamps one build id into `sw.js`, `index.html` and `js/build.js`; if a stale page ever meets newer scripts, the app resets its offline copy and reloads itself once.
  - Self-hosted fonts.
  - An install button (`beforeinstallprompt`) with iOS, Android and desktop instructions. It's never shown when the app already runs installed (display-mode standalone, fullscreen, minimal-ui or window-controls-overlay, iOS `navigator.standalone`, or an `android-app://` referrer), and it's decided before first paint so it can't flash. The `appinstalled` event is remembered in localStorage, so a normal browser tab hides it too; Chrome/Edge's `navigator.getInstalledRelatedApps()` is used as an extra hint (the manifest lists this web app in `related_applications` with its `id`). Dismissing the browser's install prompt hides the button for 90 days; on iPhone the Add to Home Screen hint shows until you tap "Got it" once. If the app is uninstalled, the next `beforeinstallprompt` clears the flag and the button returns.
  - An update banner when a new version is deployed.
  - An Android share target: share videos from the Gallery straight into the installed app.

- **Keyboard:** shortcuts only act when you aren't typing, and a focused control keeps its own keys (arrows move a focused slider, Space presses a focused button). Every slider has a label and announces its current value. Press Tab to reach the timeline items: Enter selects, ←/→ nudges by a frame (Shift: 1 s; main clips swap with their neighbour), Alt+←/→ trims the end. Tabs support arrow keys. `[` and `]` lower and raise the selected item's volume (see above).

## Browser support for export
Only Chrome was tested (desktop, plus a phone-sized viewport with touch emulation). The Safari and Firefox rows describe the expected behaviour, based on the capability detection built into the app.

| Browser | Engine used | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Chrome / Edge 94+ (desktop, Android) | WebCodecs (fast) | **MP4**: H.264 video. Audio is AAC where the browser has an AAC encoder (Windows, macOS, Android); on Linux Chrome it's **Opus-in-MP4** (YouTube accepts both) |
| Safari 17+ (macOS / iOS) | WebCodecs if both VideoEncoder and AudioEncoder exist, otherwise real-time MediaRecorder | MP4 |
| Firefox | WebCodecs (VP9/Opus WebM or H.264 MP4 when available), otherwise MediaRecorder | WebM (or MP4) |

The real-time fallback takes as long as the video itself. Keep the tab visible while it runs.

## Deploying
1. After editing any file, run `python3 bump-version.py`. It stamps `sw.js` with a content hash so installed copies pick up the update.
2. Upload the whole folder to any static HTTPS host (GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, and so on). Every path is relative, so a sub-path such as `/user.github.io/editor/` works too.
3. Open the URL on your phone:
   - **Android (Chrome):** tap *Install app*.
   - **iPhone (Safari):** Share → *Add to Home Screen*.

To test locally, run `python3 -m http.server 8000` in this folder and open http://localhost:8000. Service workers need HTTPS or localhost.

Development checks (optional, needs Node 20+): `npm install`, then `npm run lint` (ESLint), `npm test` (unit tests in `tests/`), or `npm run predeploy` to lint, test and bump the version in one go. `node_modules` is git-ignored and isn't needed by the app.

## Notes and limitations
- **ffmpeg.wasm isn't included.** The single-threaded core is about 30 MB. It encodes H.264 far slower than real time on phones, and it would need to be downloaded before the first offline use. The WebCodecs path already produces MP4 faster than real time, so ffmpeg.wasm added little except weight.
- **Storage:** exports stream to disk where the browser supports OPFS writable streams or the File System Access save picker (Chrome/Edge, Android Chrome, recent Safari/Firefox for OPFS). Elsewhere they're assembled in memory. Media is stored in the browser's IndexedDB. Clearing site data deletes it. The app asks for persistent storage when you import media.
- **Speed changes:** in the export, audio for sped-up or slowed-down clips is time-stretched with WSOLA so the pitch stays the same. Preview uses the browser's own pitch preservation.
- **HEVC phone videos** play in the preview wherever the browser can play them. If WebCodecs can't decode them, export falls back to a slower frame-by-frame seek of a `<video>` element; output is identical, just slower.
- **Color grading** uses a small WebGL shader, so it looks the same in every browser. If WebGL is unavailable or the GPU context is lost, it falls back to canvas filters with an approximation of warmth, fade and vignette, and returns to WebGL when the context is restored.
- **Security:** the page ships a Content-Security-Policy (only the app's own scripts, plus wasm for the HEIC decoder and the speech engine; `connect-src ... https:` for link import, which is needed because the addresses are typed by the user; no frames are allowed). Imported project files are validated: settings are clamped and embedded media must be real base64 media data (URLs are never fetched). `window.__app` is only exposed on localhost or with `?debug`.
- **Several tabs:** cleanup of unused stored media is coordinated between open tabs (Web Locks + BroadcastChannel), so one tab never deletes media another tab still has in its undo history.
- **Voiceover** needs microphone permission and a secure context (HTTPS or localhost). Recordings are Opus/WebM in Chrome and Firefox and AAC/MP4 in Safari. Use headphones if you turn off "Silence timeline audio while recording".
- **Chroma key** works best on evenly lit, saturated green or blue backgrounds. Hair-fine detail is approximated with a soft edge.
- **Keyframe times** are relative to the item's start, so moving a clip or text moves its animation with it. Trimming an item's start shifts its keyframes, so the animation stays pinned to the same frames.
- **Long media:** waveforms and export audio are decoded by streaming, so an hour-long sermon isn't held in memory as raw audio. Preview audio plays from the media element.
- **Animated GIFs** are decoded with the browser's ImageDecoder where available (Chrome, Edge, Safari 17+ partly), otherwise with the bundled gifuct-js. Very large GIFs are downscaled to limit memory, and animation is capped at 60 s per clip by default. If a GIF can't be decoded, it's used as a still image and you're told.
- **HEIC:** only the primary image of a HEIC file is used. The wasm decoder is 1.4 MB and is precached for offline use.
- **Loop seams** in the preview are accurate to about a screen frame. In the export they're sample-accurate, with a short crossfade dip.

## Integrations: limits

- **No arbitrary control.** The editor cannot log in to, script, read or click inside another site or app. Integration means files and links moving in and out: the share sheet, downloads, a picker, or a direct file link.
- **CORS.** Link import only works for files whose server sends `Access-Control-Allow-Origin` for other sites (many CDNs and storage buckets do; most video sites, Drive and Dropbox page links do not). Links to web pages, YouTube/Vimeo pages and HLS playlists are not media files and are refused with an explanation.
- **Share target support.** Web Share Target works where the installed web app is supported (Chrome/Edge on Android and desktop). iPhone Safari cannot receive shares; use Add from Drive, Photos or Files.
- **Memory.** A linked file is held in memory while it downloads, hence the size limit.

Local test servers: `tools/linkserver.py` (HTTPS media with/without CORS, redirects, slow and oversize responses) and `tools/pagesserver.py` (serves `/tmp/site` with GitHub Pages cache headers, for the upgrade test `tools/upgrade-connect.mjs`).
